Privacy and ads

51 Mimi Games is a free set of mini-games made for kids and families. This page says what the site stores and how ads work.

What the site keeps

  • Your game progress, settings and keys are stored in your own browser (localStorage). We do not read it.
  • If you make an account, the server stores the name you chose, a scrambled version of your password, and the scores, settings and keys you save to it. There is no email requirement.
  • The server's normal logs can include your device's network address for a short time, which is used to limit abuse (for example too many login attempts).
  • There is no tracking or analytics of our own on this site.

Ads

Some copies of this site show ads, which help pay for running it. Ads are never shown until you press Show ads in the notice, and No thanks means nothing is loaded from an ad network. You can change your mind at any time with Ad choices under the banner on the game menu.

  • Ads are requested as child-directed and non-personalised: they are not chosen from what you did on other sites, and the ad network is told this content is for children.
  • There are no ads while you are playing a game. A short video ad can appear between games, and you can choose to watch a video to earn keys.
  • When ads are on, the ad network (Google AdSense) loads from its own servers and may set cookies or similar identifiers needed to show and measure ads. Google explains this at policies.google.com/technologies/ads.

Parents

If you would like ads off for a child, choose No thanks (or Ad choices then No thanks). The games work exactly the same.

This page describes how the software behaves by default. Whoever runs a particular copy of the site is responsible for its own settings and for checking that its ad network account and local laws are met.
